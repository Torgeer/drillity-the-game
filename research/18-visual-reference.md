# 18 — Visual reference: cross-section piling animation

The project owner shared this as a visual target:
`https://www.facebook.com/reel/2949511582048564` — *Civil Engineering*,
**"5 Types Piling Process Cross Section View"**, credited to Qaisar Malik.

**Caveat, stated up front: Facebook labels this AI-generated content**
("AI-innehåll"), and the on-screen title in the frame I captured reads
*"Reverse Rotary drilling clyp"* — misspelled. **Treat nothing in it as an
engineering source.** Its value is the **visual language**, and on that it is a
very good target. For anything technical, use `research/01`–`17`.

---

## The single most important observation

**Surface and cross-section are ONE CONTINUOUS SCENE.** The ground plane cuts
straight across the frame; above it a crawler piling rig with its mast, below it
the same tool continuing down the hole through the strata. Same lighting, same
perspective, same air. **There is no divider, no seam, no visible split.** The
cut face simply *is* the ground, opened.

`GAMEDESIGN.md` §1 already says the game's two bands are "one continuous world
fiction: the borehole in the section lines up horizontally with the mast above
it." **This reference is what that sentence looks like when it is achieved.**
The game renders two scissored viewports with a seam between them, and the
degree to which that seam is invisible is the difference between this image and
a diagram with a picture stuck on top.

---

## What else the frame gets right

**The strata are the hero, and they read as real geology.** Four distinct
horizons, top to bottom:

1. brown worked fill / topsoil, with a compacted trafficked surface
2. a **cobble and gravel horizon** — visibly *rounded* stones of varied size in
   a sandy matrix, individually legible
3. grey-blue **banded silt/clay** with fine horizontal bedding
4. **fractured, blocky rock** at the base

Each has its own colour, its own grain size and its own internal structure, and
the **contacts between them are irregular** — they wander, they are not ruled
lines. That irregularity is most of what sells it.

**The site has depth.** A crawler piling rig in the foreground *and* two
excavators plus plant in the mid-ground, on a genuinely worked surface with
track marks. Not one machine alone on an empty plane — which is what the game
currently renders.

**The tool is fully readable in the cut** — the casing runs the height of the
section with its own surface detail, so the eye follows it from the mast into
the ground.

**Lighting is photographic**: bright overcast daylight, soft directional
shadows, a believable sky, and the cut face lit as if genuinely open to it.

**The UI is one title label.** Everything else on screen is the render. This is
the same instruction the owner gave directly — *"PREMIUM !! no overlapping and
the most simply UI we can have"* — arrived at from the other direction.

---

## Actionable, in priority order

1. **Kill the seam.** The two bands must read as one cut. Matching horizon,
   matching light direction and matching aerial perspective across the boundary
   matter more than anything happening inside either band.
2. **Irregular, wandering stratum contacts** — never a straight horizontal rule.
3. **Cobbles and clasts that are individually legible** in the coarse horizons,
   at a size that survives the real band height on a phone.
4. **Populate the mid-ground of the surface site.** A second machine and site
   plant give the scene depth; a lone rig reads as a product shot.
5. **A worked, trafficked ground surface** — tracks, spoil, disturbed material —
   not clean terrain with a machine placed on it.

Point 4 pairs directly with the site-archetype work: an `urban-plot` should have
a concrete pump and a cage crane in the mid-ground, an `open-pit-bench` a haul
road above and a face below. The archetype decides *what* stands there; this
reference is about *that something does*.
