## 0. How to use this file

Every photograph listed in §2–§4 was **OPENED and looked at** — the observations
are what is actually in the frame, not what the filename suggests. That is the
honesty convention and it is absolute: an entry marked **OPENED** means the image
was put in front of my own eyes; anything not opened is marked **NOT OPENED**
with the reason, and the only such items are the 74 pattern-rejects in §5, which
are listed by pattern precisely so nobody re-opens them. Nothing here is inferred
from a filename.

Read §1 to find the one best image for your machine, §2 for the per-photo notes,
§3–§4 for the ground and the surface finishes that make a render look
photographed rather than modelled, §5 so you do not waste an hour on rejects, and
**§6 — the most important section, because it names the machines that have no
photograph at all and are therefore being modelled blind.**

Viewpoint is stated for every entry, because **an elevation is worth several
three-quarters**. `ELEVATION` = proportions can be taken off it directly.
`3/4` = silhouette and massing only. `DETAIL` = one component close up.
`CAD` = a shaded solid-model view (clean geometry, no branding, no wear).
`SCREEN` = a photograph of a monitor, or a screenshot (usable, but soft).

**The headline finding: the library is a tooling library, not a machine
library.** It is extraordinarily strong on bits, crowns, ring bits, casing and
threads, and it contains one genuinely excellent working-site sequence. Eleven of
the eighteen machine ids have nothing usable at all.

**→ §7 is a separate, self-contained section covering the `Atpa\` and
`Atpa\Atpa products\` subfolders (93 images) and the five `ATPA-*.pdf` photo sets
in `Downloads\` (23 pages).** They are excluded from the §1–§6 scope and tally
above. §7 is a **drilling-TOOL manufacturer's own factory and product
photography**: augers, casing shoes, drill heads, casing, picks and carbide, at
four stages of manufacture. It adds no machine reference of any kind — every
machine gap in §6 stands — but it is the best source in the library for **auger
geometry, weld and heat-tint colour, paint finish, round-shank picks, and how
tooling is stacked, strapped and shipped**, and it partly closes §6's
`tools-anchors-sda` gap. Its honesty convention, viewpoint codes and badge
flagging are the same as this section's.

#### The CAD set — clean geometry, no branding, no wear

Six shaded solid-model screenshots, all OPENED. `15.14.23`/`15.16.x` and

## 4. Materials and wear — with the photograph each came from

- **Fresh weld against oxidised parent metal is the strongest contrast on any drilling
  tool.** In `WhatsApp Image 2026-06-01 at 11.46.36.jpeg` the weld beads at the base of
  every bar stub are **bright silver-white and entirely un-rusted**, standing proud in a
  rippled bead, while the casting around them is dark blue-grey mill scale under orange
  rust. Two materials, one part. In the `2026-08-06 at 10.49.22` set the same beads are
  **ringed by a small halo of orange rust** where the heat-affected zone has gone first.

  sharp edge, running in **drip streaks down the sides**, sheltered undersides still dark.

### 7.7 `Atpa\Atpa products\` — 51 studio photographs of loose tooling

All **51 files OPENED**. `cfa-rig.md` dismisses this whole folder in one line as
"~30 WhatsApp images of loose tooling"; there are **51**, and they are **a
deliberate product-photography session**: every item shot alone, in daylight, on
a **dark grey carpet mat over rust-and-slate mottled floor tiles**, in the
company's own office/showroom (a wall-mounted TV, brushed-metal cabinets and a
white wall appear in the wider frames). **They are the best isolated-component
references in the entire research library** — no clutter, no dirt, one object per
frame, most of them near-elevations.

**The single most important fact about this folder: nearly every tool in it is
finished in a strong ULTRAMARINE BLUE**, and the exceptions are bare machined
steel. That is a complete, self-consistent tool-colour scheme and the game could
adopt it wholesale (with the maker's marks removed).

#### The universal drive interface — worth reading first

Across the whole folder **one drive interface repeats on every small tool**:

- **A plain male HEXAGONAL shank with ONE round cross hole for a locking pin**,
  bare bright machined steel, chamfered at the end, often with a small
  centre-drill dimple in the end face and a **warm straw/gold tint on the lit
  flats** against cool grey on the shaded ones.
- The paint stops at the shank shoulder — **the shank is always bare**, on every
  one of the twenty-odd tools that use it.
- Larger tools instead use a **bare, coarse rope/API pin thread** (`12.21.27`,
  `28 (2)–(4)`, `30 (5)`, `31`, `43`), and the very largest use a **female hex
  socket** (§7.2, §7.6).

**If the game has one tool-attachment mechanic, this is what it looks like: a
hex stub and a pin.** That is a genuinely useful, brand-free system detail.

#### Round-shank picks — a five-size ladder, one per frame

**`… 12.21.20 (4).jpeg`, `12.21.21.jpeg`, `12.21.21 (1).jpeg`,
`12.21.21 (2).jpeg`, `12.21.21 (3).jpeg`** — all OPENED. Five picks, five sizes,
each alone and near-elevation. Together with the loose picks in
`13.53.05 (8).jpeg` (§7.6) this is the **best pick reference in the library**.

- Common form, top to bottom: **a ballistic/conical tip → a stepped shoulder
  collar carrying two or three raised turning rings → a plain cylindrical shank
  → a retaining feature at the very end.**
- **The whole pick is painted blue, TIP INCLUDED.** This matters and it
  contradicts the "carbide is never painted" rule that holds for casing shoes
  (§7.9): **a NEW round-shank pick ships fully coated; the bright carbide only
  appears after the first hour of work.** So a fresh head reads all-blue and a
  worked one reads blue with silver tips. That is a free, honest wear state.
- **A bright bare steel retaining clip shows through a slot in the coating** at
  the shank end on three of the five — a small silver rectangle on blue.
- Proportion across the ladder: **tip length ≈ 0.35 of overall; shank ≈ 0.45**;
  the smallest is squat, the largest slender *(image-scaled from
  near-elevations, ±10 %)*.

**The rules that fall out of it:**

- **Five delivered paint colours, and they are tool-type specific, not random:**
  satin anthracite (big augers) · mid-grey (some augers) · glossy near-black
  (casing drill heads and pointed heads) · glossy ochre-gold (casing cutting
  shoes and DTH bits — confirmed on two unrelated frames, `Bohrkopf_für_VdW508`
  and `656646487_…n`) · **ultramarine blue (the entire small-tool range)**.
- **On BIG product tooling the thread is never painted** and the mask line is
  hard and clean, with no feathering — the most consistent single rule in the
  set (`IMG_20230414_132933`, `Sw100`, `IMG_20250319_090942`, `13.53.06`).
  **On SMALL blue bits it is the opposite**: the paint either runs straight over
  the thread (`12.21.30 (5)`, `12.21.31`) or stops in a ragged, drippy, tarry
  line (`12.21.26 (4)`, `12.21.28 (2)`). ⚠ **Both behaviours are real. Do not
  make one of them a law.**
- **Paint goes on over the welds, and does not hide them.** A bead becomes a
  raised *coloured* rope with its ripples still countable
  (`12.21.28`, `Bohrkopf_für_VdW508`, `12.21.28 (2)`). This is the opposite of
  §4's bright-weld-against-oxide contrast, which applies to unpainted castings.
  Both are correct, for different tools.
- **Thick paint visibly ROUNDS sharp edges** and pools darker in fillets while
  thinning on arrises (`12.21.26 (3)`, `12.21.27`). New painted tooling has
  softened geometry, not crisp geometry.
- **Carbide on a casing shoe or crown is never painted** — flat-top buttons,
  pick tips and hardfaced tooth tips stay bright inside painted or blackened
  bodies. **But a NEW round-shank pick ships fully coated, tip included**
  (`12.21.20 (4)` and the four siblings), so a fresh head is all one colour and
  a worked one shows silver tips. Two states, both true.
- **Bare machined steel is not one colour.** A hex drive spigot reads warm
  straw/brass on its lit flats while the turned collar 30 mm away reads bright
  silver (`Sw100`, `13.53.06` — two independent cameras); a rope thread reads
  golden with mill oil (`IMG_20230414_132933`); a coupling reads neutral satin
  grey (`12.21.19`); a forged drive key reads matte, sugary and almost dull
  (`12.21.26 (2)`).
- **A thread has three possible finishes: bright bare, golden with mill oil, or
  dark grey phosphate** (`13.53.05 (10)`). They are not interchangeable.
- **Brazed carbide reads COPPER-ORANGE, not steel-silver** (`13.53.05 (14)`) — a
  third metal colour that appears nowhere in §2–§6.
- **A batch is not one material.** In `ATPA-Bohrkronen p.5` the button crowns
  read pale grey-white (shot-blasted) and the threaded sleeves beside them read
  dark blue-black (oiled), in the same heap.
- **Zinc-plated fasteners are the brightest thing on a finished tool** — locking
  pins and bolt heads read as small hard silver dots against matte paint.

### 7.10 Wear — where the metal actually changes, and on which face

Most of this folder is new goods, so the wear evidence is narrow but very
specific, and it is mostly *manufacturing* wear rather than service wear.

- **Heat tint is the dominant "wear" on a new drilling tool, and it is
  positional, never uniform:** a **halo around each weld bead**, strongest at the
  bead and washing out over roughly two bead-widths (`13.53.05 (1)`, `_MG_7700`);
  **patches around each insert seat** on a casing shoe (`IMG_20230414_094715`);
  a **narrow rainbow band at an upset rod end** (`_MG_7680`); a **consistent
  rainbow ring between thread and collar on every tube in a rack**
  (`13.53.05 (15)`, and unmistakable at size in `ATPA-Bearbeitete-Fotos p.2`);
  a **full oil-slick field across hex flats and flight roots** (`13.53.05 (4)`);
  **patches of pink, violet and turquoise across a whole large flight**
  (`13.53.05 (11)`); and a **dark scorch ring inside a bore** below drive-lug
  welds (`Bohrkopf_für_VdW508`).
- **Bright machined faces freckle with rust first, and they do it while the part
  is still in stock** — orange spots on otherwise mirror end faces (`_MG_7681`).
- **A replaced part is brighter than its neighbours.** One bare bright pick in an
  otherwise anthracite-painted auger head (`BS_SW80` crop). The cheapest and most
  convincing "this tool has been serviced" cue available.
- **An empty socket is as informative as a full one.** `13.53.05 (7)` shows two
  round pick holders with the picks removed, the retaining slot visible inside;
  several blue wings carry **rows of bored but unfilled button dimples**
  (`12.21.30 (5)`, `12.21.31`). A head with a gap reads as used at a glance.
- **Used hollow bars go grey-brown with orange freckling over their whole
  length, never striped** (`656646487_…n`) — unlike §4's casing tubes, which
  stripe vertically from the joint. **Threaded bar rusts evenly; smooth tube
  rusts directionally.** Both are in the library and they are different.
- **Coupler and bar wear differently because they are different finishes** — the
  galvanised band on a coupler stays bright while the bar around it browns
  (`656646487_…n`).
- **A rusty tube with a bright machined end is the strongest single contrast in
  the folder** — orange-streaked grey-blue body, mirror thread, rainbow ring
  between (`13.53.05 (15)`).
- **White paint dabs and chalk scribbles are inspection marks, not decoration** —
  on individual shoe teeth (`13.53.05 (14)`), across a big flight face
  (`13.53.05 (11)`), on casing flanks (`IMG_20240207_131749`).
- **NOT SOURCED here: any in-service wear at all.** Nothing in either Atpa folder
  or any of the PDFs shows a tool that has been down a hole — no polished gauge,
  no rounded-off buttons, no chipped carbide, no worn-flat teeth, no mud. §2 and
  §4 remain the only source for that, and this whole section is therefore a
  **"day one" reference**.
